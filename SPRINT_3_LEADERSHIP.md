## First source-backed case

### Teacher Recruitment & Evaluation System

**Proof type:** SOURCE-BACKED INSTITUTIONAL SYSTEM

Source basis includes:

- a role-specific recruitment / JD framework covering leadership, pre-primary, academic and activity positions
- explicit competencies, responsibilities and performance expectations
- staged selection processes including application screening, written/leadership evaluation where appropriate, demo/practical interaction and final interview
- a subject-teacher recruitment assessment that combines pedagogy, subject knowledge and classroom application

The science recruitment assessment includes:

- 100 questions
- 80 MCQs + 20 descriptive questions
- a dedicated Child Development & Pedagogy component
- formative/diagnostic assessment concepts
- inclusion and learner diversity
- activity-based / experiential teaching
- critical thinking
- class-level subject knowledge
- lesson-plan and classroom-application tasks

The public portfolio should not reproduce the raw recruitment pack or full test. It should show privacy-safe derivatives that prove process design and evaluation depth.

## Decision narrative

### Context

Institutional hiring is not only a vacancy-filling process. Different roles require different competencies, reporting relationships, instructional depth and performance expectations.

### Problem

Generic resume screening and a single interview do not adequately test whether a candidate can teach, lead, plan, communicate, assess students or operate inside a school system.

### My role

Institutional requirements owner, process architect and evaluation-framework designer.

### Implementation path

1. Define role scope and reporting relationship.
2. Specify qualifications, experience and core competencies.
3. Translate the role into responsibilities and expected performance.
4. Screen applications against role requirements.
5. Use written / leadership evaluation where the role requires it.
6. Observe teaching or practical interaction through demo stages.
7. Conduct structured management interview.
8. Align selection with probation and performance expectations.

### Trade-offs

- A deeper process takes more effort than a single interview but provides more evidence of role fit.
- Standardisation improves consistency, but tests and demos must remain role-specific.
- Written tests can evaluate thinking and knowledge but cannot replace observation of classroom delivery.
- Strong requirements improve accountability only if later performance review uses the same expectations.

### Failure modes

- over-weighting written scores while ignoring classroom performance
- using generic rubrics for very different roles
- testing memorisation instead of teaching judgement
- unclear interviewer scoring criteria
- allowing recruitment criteria to disappear after appointment rather than feed probation/performance review

### Current value

The institution has a source-backed structure connecting job definition, screening, evaluation, demonstration, interview and explicit performance expectations rather than treating these as unrelated HR steps.

### Next iteration

- structured scorecards for each selection stage
- interviewer calibration guidance
- evidence-based demo observation rubric
- probation review mapped back to original performance expectations
- anonymised sample scoring evidence suitable for the portfolio
